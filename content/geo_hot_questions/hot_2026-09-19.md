### 1. Why is GPS confirmation of Special Relativity?
- 来源: stackexchange/physics (https://physics.stackexchange.com/questions/876009/why-is-gps-confirmation-of-special-relativity)
- why: GPS is the one relativity result a lay reader can already picture, and the popular framing (special relativity) is wrong in an interesting way: the gravitational term dominates and the net is a gain, not a loss.
- answer_angle: Separate the two clock terms. The 1905 speed term makes the satellite clock lose time; the gravitational blueshift of sitting higher in Earth's potential makes it gain more. The published offset is a design constant, so GPS is an operational check on the clock terms, not a clean single test of SR.

### 2. Can the relativistic effects on Mercury's orbit be reproduced in Newtonian mechanics by modifying Newton's gravity force law?

- why: Freshest high-score question of the cycle. The honest answer is a real result: a velocity-dependent correction to the inverse-square law can be fitted to reproduce the observed perihelion advance, which is why the precession alone never proved general relativity.

A modified force law with velocity dependence can be tuned to the precession number; what separated GR was its other predictions (light bending, gravitational redshift, Shapiro delay) coming out of the same one-parameter theory.

### 7. Did Earth's rotational slowing down contribute to its 23.5 degree axial tilt, or was it determined by early impacts?
- 来源: stackexchange/space (https://space.stackexchange.com/questions/70708/did-earths-rotational-slowing-down-contribute-to-its-23-5-circ-axial-tilt-o)
- why: Removes a common misconception: tidal despinning changes spin rate, not the direction of the spin axis, so the tilt has to come from the formation and impact history.
- answer_angle: Angular momentum is directional, so slowing the spin changes magnitude, not the axis orientation. Tidal friction transfers spin angular momentum to the Moon's orbit; the tilt is set by the accreted and impact history. Precession moves the axis around a cone without changing the tilt angle.
