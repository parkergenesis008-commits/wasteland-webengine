# Semi-Dirac State and Directional Mass Nullification

## 1. Physical Introduction: The Disappearance of Mass
Under the dominion of classical mechanics, mass is an eternal scalar property of matter. Even relativity only allows mass to approach infinity as velocity increases. However, topological physics has torn open a loophole in condensed matter crystals (such as Topological Semimetals): due to band crossings and the formation of Dirac cones, the energy and momentum of electrons exhibit an absolute linear relationship, and their "effective mass" instantly vanishes to zero at the microscopic level.

These particles need not reach the speed of light to traverse the crystal lattice like ghosts in a massless state. Even more bizarre is the existence of "Semi-Dirac Fermions"—their mass is zero along one spatial axis, yet extremely large along the perpendicular axis.

## 2. Clarification: Effective Mass vs. Inertial Mass

Before describing the override protocol, an honest distinction is necessary. The Dirac cone in a topological semimetal renders the **effective mass** of charge carriers (electrons/holes) to zero inside the lattice — this is a band-structure concept, defined as the curvature of the energy-momentum dispersion: 1/m*_eff_ = ∂²E/∂k². An electron in a Dirac cone behaves as a massless relativistic particle *within the crystal*, but its inertial mass (the m₀ ≈ 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg rest mass) remains unchanged — it is a fundamental particle property, not a band parameter.

Extending this effective-mass-zeroing to macroscopic **inertial mass** (the m in F = ma that governs momentum, kinetic energy, and G-force tolerance) requires a fundamentally different mechanism: it is not simply the same condensed-matter band-structure effect scaled up. In the Shepherd's Wasteland architecture, this extension is the function of the **Baryonic Torsion Engine operating in Mode C (Mass Nullification)** — see *Cooperative Resonance and Torsion Compression*, §5. In Mode C, the torsion field is modulated to match a Semi-Dirac effective-mass tensor, but the coupling from microscopic band-structure effective mass to macroscopic inertial mass requires the N² superradiance amplification chain (§4.5 of the same document) and a phase-locked loop tuned to the inertial frame rather than the lattice dispersion. The lore below describes the *desired effect* of this mode; the physical gap between effective mass and inertial mass is bridged by the torsion engine's multistep coupling, not by a literal macroscopic Dirac cone.

## 3. Dirac Mass Override Protocol
In the armament sequence of *Shepherd's Wasteland*, simply solving "anti-gravity (gravitational deflection)" still leaves the wearer crushed by their own "inertial mass" during supersonic maneuvers. Therefore, the Computational Arena introduced mass nullification technology.

Through baryonic torsion fields and QM-Tether armor, the system forcibly renders an equivalent "topological semimetal" band structure in the macroscopic space surrounding the wearer. Within this artificial Dirac cone field, the "effective inertial mass" of the wearer's macroscopic body is forcibly redefined to zero by the system's underlying code.

This means that an extremely tiny physical thrust (such as a simple muscular push) can generate near-infinite instantaneous acceleration. And when the wearer executes a 100-Mach acute-angle turn, being massless, they are immune to any G-force tearing.

The kinetic energy is unceremoniously devoured by the absolute inertial wall, shattering into its own debris.

## 5. Conclusion: Mass as a Variable
In the high-dimensional engine of Reality-as-Code, mass is no longer an intrinsic property bestowed upon objects by God. It is merely a scalar parameter mounted on a three-dimensional grid. Having understood the crossing rules of topology, mass can be arbitrarily nullified to zero or endlessly multiplied.

**Q: 2026 Status of Higgs coupling constants**
<p><em>asked on stackexchange/physics · physics 2026-10-12 official API recheck: 1 vote, 0 answers, 72 views, open (posted 2026-10-11)</em></p>

In the Standard Model each Higgs coupling is fixed to be proportional to that particle's mass, so the live question is whether measured couplings agree with those predictions. At the LHC they are extracted from production and decay rates, and the pattern is that couplings to heavy bosons and the top quark are constrained to a few percent while couplings to light fermions remain poorly constrained. Public combinations keep reporting agreement with the Standard Model within uncertainties, with no confirmed deviation, so a "2026 status" is best read as an updated uncertainty table rather than a discovery claim. Anyone quoting this should take the current numbers from the Particle Data Group and the latest LHC combinations; this answer deliberately quotes no figures. In our fiction, <a href='semi-dirac-mass-nullification.html'>semi-Dirac mass nullification</a> is a speculative setting in which an engineered lattice makes an effective mass vanish; it is **not** a claim about real Higgs physics.

**Q: How can the hydrogen ground state have zero angular momentum if the Bohr radius is nonzero?**
<p><em>asked on stackexchange/physics · 0 points, 3 answers, 103 views (StackExchange API, 2026-10-18), open</em></p>

The ground state carries no orbital angular momentum because its wavefunction is spherically symmetric: no axis is singled out, so there is no rotation to quantize, and the electron's kinetic energy is radial instead. The Bohr radius is not the radius of a circular orbit but the most probable distance in a radial distribution, so a finite size and zero angular momentum are fully consistent. **In the setting of this page**, the point is about which quantity may be called mass: band curvature can drive the effective mass of a carrier to zero along chosen directions, a behaviour reported for semi-Dirac dispersions in a few materials and the same band-curvature logic that <a href='artificial-kondo-lattice.html'>engineered lattices</a> exploit, while inertial mass stays untouched and its nullification is speculative fiction.
