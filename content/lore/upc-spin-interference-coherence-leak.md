## Plain-language analogy

> Imagine two massive ships passing each other at sea, their hulls never touching, yet the waves between them produce a perfect interference pattern. The STAR experiment found: when gold nuclei "graze" past each other at large distances (impact parameter > 2R), the J/ψ particles produced solely through photon exchange exhibit spin interference patterns in their decay electrons. This is like the waves stirred up by two ships being able to "remember" the direction of their passing — not classical water waves, but a leakage of quantum coherence.

## Phenomenon

STAR Collaboration (2026) reported in PRL 136, 242302 the first experimental evidence of **spin interference** in exclusive J/ψ → e⁺e⁻ photoproduction in ultraperipheral Au+Au collisions (√s_NN = 200 GeV).

Key findings:
1. **Negative cos(2φ) modulation**: The azimuthal distribution of the electron-pair decay plane exhibits a structural modulation, with λ < 0
2. **Interference source**: Quantum coherent superposition of the polarization states of quasi-real photons exchanged by the two gold nuclei
3. **Impact parameter**: impact parameter > 2R ≈ 14 fm, nucleons do not touch, interaction occurs solely via photon exchange
4. **Ultraperipheral Collisions (UPC)**: The dominant process is photon-Pomeron/gluon fusion, producing the J/ψ vector meson

This negative cos(2φ) modulation cannot be explained classically — it requires quantum entanglement between the two photon polarization channels, representing a **quantum coherence signature** demonstrated over ultra-long distances (nuclear scale).
