# Crossed Surface Flat Bands — Quantum Channels in Three-Dimensional Superconducting Altermagnets

## Plain-Language Analogy

> Imagine a three-story parking garage where, on the middle level, a perfectly horizontal "zero-slope channel" appears, allowing vehicles to glide without resistance. Interestingly, this channel exists only on the surface of the garage, and the spin arrangements on the floors above and below are oppositely aligned—this is the crossed flat bands in altermagnetic superconductors, a natural topological quantum highway.

In simple terms: on the surface of a specially designed material, electrons can move along specific "zigzag paths" without energy loss, and these paths are strictly protected by symmetry.

## Core Physical Mechanism (Phenomenon)

Discovered by Fukaya et al. (PRL 136, 226001), in **three-dimensional superconducting altermagnets**, surface states form **zero-energy crossed surface flat bands**. Core physics:

- **Altermagnetism**: An emerging magnetic phase where the magnetic moments of two sublattices in the crystal lattice are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction, similar to antiferromagnetism (net magnetization = 0), but differing in that the spin-splitting in altermagnets exhibits d-wave/g-wave symmetry in k-space
- **Synergy with superconductivity**: The d-wave superconducting pairing symmetry combined with altermagnetic spin-splitting produces nontrivial topology, forming zero-energy Majorana-like flat bands on the surface
- **Crossed structure**: Two flat bands cross along specific directions in the surface Brillouin zone, forming symmetry-protected linear crossing points
