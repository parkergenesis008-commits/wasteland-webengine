# Long-Range Coupling BKT Topological Phase Transition Persistence

## Plain-Language Analogy

> *"This is like two people skating on a frozen lake, where cracks begin to appear on the ice surface (vortex-antivortex pairs). An ordinary ice surface (short-range coupling) shatters when the temperature rises slightly—cracks are scattered everywhere. But if you embed a wire mesh beneath the ice (long-range coupling), even at higher temperatures the cracks hold each other together, and the ice surface becomes more persistent."*
>
> The essence of the BKT transition is the binding and unbinding of vortices. Long-range coupling is like adding an invisible elastic rope to each vortex—the longer the rope, the greater the energy required to break it.

## Physical Phenomenon

**Core Mechanism**: The Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transition is a topological phase transition in two-dimensional systems driven by the binding/unbinding of vortex-antivortex pairs. This paper investigates the persistence of the BKT transition in the XY model under long-range algebraic decaying coupling (decay exponent α).

Key finding: When the decay exponent α of the long-range coupling decreases from infinity (purely nearest-neighbor) to 2.0, the BKT transition temperature increases, and topological order persists over a wider temperature range. When α is sufficiently small, long-range coupling even completely suppresses vortex unbinding, keeping the system topologically ordered at any temperature.

Physical reason: Long-range coupling increases the interaction energy of vortex-antivortex pairs, requiring higher thermal excitation energy for unbinding. This effectively provides "additional stability" for the topologically ordered phase.

### Failure Cause Analysis

The Berry curvature generated by BKT vortex phase winding is inherently **topologically protected**—the vortex phase is an integer multiple of 2π, and this quantization limits the amplitude of the Berry curvature. Compared to Kagome flat-band systems, XY vortices lack the electronic density-of-states enhancement provided by the flat band structure, so spin torque cannot couple to an effective metric perturbation.
